H-1B Specialty Occupation Workers

The H-1B classification applies to people who wish to work in the United States in a
specialty occupation that requires at least a bachelor's degree or its equivalent.

Cap and registration: Most H-1B petitions are subject to an annual numerical cap. USCIS
uses an electronic registration process; prospective petitioners (employers) register
during a designated registration period and, if selected, may file an H-1B cap-subject
petition. Some petitions are cap-exempt (for example, certain employers such as
institutions of higher education and affiliated nonprofits).

Cap-gap: An F-1 student who is the beneficiary of a timely filed H-1B cap-subject petition
requesting a change of status with an October 1 start date may have their F-1 status and
any current employment authorization extended until the start of the H-1B period. This is
known as "cap-gap."

Period of stay: H-1B status is generally granted for up to three years and may be extended,
generally to a maximum of six years, subject to exceptions.

Grace period: A nonimmigrant worker whose employment ends may be eligible for a grace
period of up to 60 days.
